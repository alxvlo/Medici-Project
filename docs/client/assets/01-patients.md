## Before you start

**Style.** Match your character sheet: soft cel-shading, dark-brown outlines, muted earthy colours,
flat lighting, no heavy gradients.

**Format.** PNG, transparent background. **Draw at twice the size listed** — "300 x 600" means send
a 600 x 1200 file. Name each file exactly as shown, lowercase, no spaces; a typo means the game
shows a grey box instead. Sizes are guides — only the aspect ratio matters much.

The game screen is 960 x 640, landscape.

---

## What changed on 10 September 2026

**These now come from your case database, not from the character sheet.** The old list had six
patients that I had invented to fit six draft cases. Your twenty cases have real patients with real
ages and body types, so the six drafts are gone.

**Twenty cases, but only 12 drawings.** This is worth knowing before the number worries you. A
patient file is named by age group, sex, and body habitus, so any two cases that share all three
share one drawing. Your twenty cases collapse to 12 unique people. Level 2's twenty-year-old man
and level 20's forty-three-year-old are both an adult male with a slight build, so they are one
picture.

That means each drawing appears in more than one case. **Draw them neutral and unremarkable** — no
detail that ties a person to one specific injury or story, because the same figure walks in for a
chest case and a skull case.

**The gap in the old list is closed.** The last version noted that no patient on your character sheet
was hypersthenic, so the game could not teach the body type that most obviously demands a technique
change. Your database has five hypersthenic cases. It is now teachable.

## About the names

Each file is named by **age group**, **sex**, and **body habitus**, in that order, rather than by a
nickname. Those three are what actually change how an exam is done, and naming files this way means
the game can work out what a picture is without me maintaining a separate list.

Age groups are: **pediatric** under 18, **adult** 18 to 64, **geriatric** 65 and over. These come
from the ages in your own database.
