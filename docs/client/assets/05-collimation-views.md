## What changed on 10 September 2026

The game went from five levels to twenty, but **this list only goes from 5 files to 12**, not to 20.

A collimation view is named for the body region and the projection, so every level that shares both
shares one picture. Your four PA chest levels — the pleural effusion, the pneumothorax, the scimitar
syndrome, and the tuberculosis — all use the same `collim-chest-pa`. What differs between those cases
is the film and the field size, not the view of the patient on the table.

That does mean **each picture serves several cases**, so keep them free of anything that would tie a
view to one specific injury.

## What these are for

The same body region as the positioning step, but now **already positioned** on the table or at the
wall stand, and seen **from the tube's point of view** — straight down for anything on the table,
straight on for anything at the wall stand.

The game draws a translucent collimation light rectangle over the top of this, plus a red target
marker, and the student turns two knobs to size the light field to the target. So the picture needs
to sit still and read clearly underneath a lit rectangle: **even lighting, no strong highlights, and
the anatomy filling most of the frame.**

The student may keep adjusting until the field is right, so this is the one picture they will look at
for a while. That is also why I would rather you drew it: it is on screen long enough for anything
odd about it to be noticed.

## About the names

`collim-<region>-<projection>` — each one matches the **correct** position for the levels that use
it, so the projection here should always match the correct entry in list 04.
