will repeat between levels the same way correct ones do.

## What these are for

**This is the most important list after the films.** Three per level: one correct position and two
wrong ones, shown side by side as buttons. The student picks one, gets **a single attempt**, and if
they pick wrong the game explains why that position fails and then shows them the correct one.

That makes these pictures the question itself. The student has to be able to tell the three apart at
a glance and reason about the difference — so the thing that distinguishes each position (whether the
palm is up or down, whether the patient is upright or lying down, whether the chin is raised) needs
to be the clearest thing in the drawing.

They appear small as buttons, about 240 x 240, and enlarge when the student hovers or holds one.
**Keep them readable at both sizes.** Use the patient for that level, on the table or at the wall
stand.

**Why I would rather you drew these.** I can generate a room. I cannot reliably generate the
difference between a limb turned fifteen degrees inward and one turned outward, and if I get it wrong
the game teaches the wrong thing while looking perfectly fine. If you hand me this list I will still
do it, but I will send every set back to you to check before it goes in.

## About the names

`pose-<region>-<projection>-<position>`, where the last part is how the patient is placed — upright
at the wall stand (`erect`), lying face-up on the table (`supine`), or the limb laid on the table
(`table`).

**Please check the last part on every row.** Your database names the projection for all twenty cases
but only states the patient's position for four of them — the two abdomens, the KUB, and the
gallbladder case. For the other 16 I have filled in the position that is standard for that
examination, and marked each one with a dagger. A dagger means *this is my assumption, not your
words*. If any is wrong, say so, because the filename changes with it.
