## What these are for — please read this one

**These three pictures per level are the game's feedback.** Everything else decorates; these teach.

The student sets the kVp and mAs on the console and gets **one attempt**. The console does not tell them whether they were right — no green light, no explanation, nothing. They collimate, they press the exposure button, and the film that appears is the first honest answer they get:

- Both values within range → the **good** film.
- Either value set too low → the **underexposed** film.
- Either value set too high → the **overexposed** film.

Then, and only then, the review screen names the correct values and explains what went wrong.

So the three versions have to be **unmistakably different at a glance**, and the difference has to be the *right* one: the underexposed film should be noisy and lacking penetration in a way that genuinely hides the pathology, and the overexposed one burnt out in a way that loses the fine detail. If a student can still read the fracture off the bad films, the lesson does not land.

Two things this does *not* depend on, which may save you work: the student's **position** choice never changes the film, and neither does their collimation. Three films per level is the whole set.
