## This is now sixty files, and that needs saying plainly

Your case database took the game from five levels to twenty. Three films per level means sixty
images, up from fifteen. **This is far and away the largest thing on your plate**, and it is the only
list where the count rose with the level count instead of being absorbed by shared files.

I looked hard for a way to reduce it and there isn't an honest one. Films cannot be shared between
levels the way the patients and room views can: levels 1, 2, 3, and 16 are all a PA chest, but a
pleural effusion film cannot stand in for a pneumothorax. Each case needs its own three.

**Thank you for confirming you will provide the overexposed and underexposed versions.** That was the
open question, and it settles the one thing that could have compromised the teaching.

**Nothing here blocks me.** Any film that has not arrived shows as a labelled grey box, so the game
is playable and we can both see exactly what is still owed. Send them in any order — one complete
level at a time is more useful than sixty optimal films with no bad ones, because a complete level
can be played and checked end to end.

## What these are for — please read this one

**These three pictures per level are the game's feedback.** Everything else decorates; these teach.

The student sets the kVp and mAs on the console and gets **one attempt**. The console does not tell
them whether they were right — no green light, no explanation, nothing. They collimate, they press
the exposure button, and the film that appears is the first honest answer they get:

- Both values within range -> the **optimal** film.
- Either value set too low -> the **under** film.
- Either value set too high -> the **over** film.

Then, and only then, the review screen names the correct values and explains what went wrong.

So the three versions have to be **unmistakably different at a glance**, and the difference has to be
the *right* one: the underexposed film should be noisy and lacking penetration in a way that
genuinely hides the pathology, and the overexposed one burnt out in a way that loses the fine detail.
If a student can still read the finding off the bad films, the lesson does not land.

Two things this does *not* depend on, which may save you work: the student's **position** choice
never changes the film, and neither does their collimation. Three films per level is the whole set.

**Why I won't generate these.** Everywhere else, a generated stand-in that is slightly wrong is
cosmetic. Here it would be a plausible-looking radiograph with anatomy that does not exist, teaching
a student to recognise something that isn't real — and it would look convincing enough that neither
of us would catch it. A grey box is more honest than a fake film. Real anonymised images are the
ideal.

## About the names

`xray-<region>-<projection>-<pathology>-<exposure>`, where exposure is `under`, `optimal`, or `over`.

**The pathology is new in this version** and it is why the names are longer. With five levels the
region and projection were enough to tell two films apart. With twenty they are not — four of your
levels are a PA chest — so the name now carries the condition as well.
